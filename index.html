Abstract

My doctoral research at the Laboratoire Ondes et Matière d'Aquitaine (UMR 5798, CNRS) established effective multi-orbital models for the electronic structure of layered nickelates. By isolating the mechanisms that distinguish nickelate physics from the cuprate paradigm, these models successfully identified antiferromagnetic and charge-order instabilities driven by Fermi-surface nesting.

My core methodology centers on mapping complex, multiscale phenomena onto rigorous, computable mathematical architectures. Whether building physics-informed translation layers that bridge Density Functional Theory (DFT) with continuum solvers, or mapping complex constraints to Ising Hamiltonians for quantum-inspired optimization, the engineering standard remains constant: rigorous mathematical abstraction validated by ground-truth data.

These computational pipelines are engineered for scale and production. Every layer-from bare-metal optimization of the numerical loops to statistical inference engines-is automated, benchmarked against exact baselines, and strictly reproducible. The numerical simulations below demonstrate working implementations of this framework across both correlated electron physics and algorithmic optimization.

Quantum-Inspired Portfolio Optimizer

Physics An optimal-portfolio problem is a ground-state problem. Assets map to spins, held or not held; expected returns to local fields \(h_i\); covariances to couplings \(J_{ij}\); the target portfolio size to a constraint term. Risk aversion \(\lambda\) weighs the couplings against the fields, and the optimum is the lowest-energy configuration of the resulting Ising Hamiltonian.

Simulation At left: ten demonstration assets as spins, flipping under Metropolis dynamics while simulated annealing lowers the temperature. The trace shows the energy relaxing to the ground state, and the converged basket appears with its return, volatility, and Sharpe ratio. Set \(K\) and \(\lambda\), then optimize. The full application adds sector constraints and annealing telemetry; its production constraint encoding stays proprietary.

Nickelate Fermiology

Physics Layered nickelates sit next to the cuprate superconductors in composition but not in their physics. In the two-band model from my doctoral research (Journal of Physics: Condensed Matter 37, 125703, 2025), an interlayer orbital hybridizes with the Ni \(d_{x^2-y^2}\) band and creates a second Fermi pocket; how that pocket emerges under doping and pressure controls the material's antiferromagnetic and charge-order tendencies.

Simulation At right: the zero-frequency spectral weight \(A(\mathbf{k},0)\) over the full Brillouin zone. Raise \(\mu\) to pull the interlayer band through the Fermi level and open the electron pocket at \(\Gamma\); increase \(V\) to hybridize the bands and gap their crossings; adjust \(t'\) to reshape the surface.

Moiré Interferometer

Physics Stacking two atomic lattices with a relative twist \(\theta\) produces a moiré superlattice whose period diverges as \(\theta \to 0\). Near the magic angle of about 1.1° the superlattice flattens the electronic bands, and flat bands mean strong correlations: superconducting and insulating phases in twisted bilayer graphene. My density-functional study of graphene on hexagonal boron nitride examined exactly this band reconstruction.
