The Short Version

I am a computational condensed matter physicist. I did my doctoral work at the Laboratoire Ondes et Matière d'Aquitaine (CNRS, Université de Bordeaux) on infinite-layer nickelates, a family of materials in which superconductivity was first observed in 2019. Nickelates share their crystal structure and nominal electron count with the cuprates, the best-known high-temperature superconductors, yet experiments find clearly different behavior. Explaining that difference was the subject of my thesis.

The central result: nickelates host a second population of conduction electrons that the cuprates lack, and this extra Fermi pocket drives their antiferromagnetic and charge-order tendencies. I established this by constructing effective multi-orbital models, reduced descriptions that keep only the electronic ingredients controlling the physics and treat those exactly. The work is published in J. Phys.: Condensed Matter (2025), the full thesis is open access, and the second simulation below reproduces the model, so you can open the pocket yourself.

Since the thesis, my work has broadened to problems that share the same mathematical structure: neural-network representations of quantum states, physics-based models of battery aging, and optimization methods borrowed from statistical mechanics. The discipline stays constant across all of them: reduce the system to the variables that control it, formulate them precisely, compute them fast, and validate against independent data. The simulations below show that loop in action.

Quantum-Inspired Portfolio Optimizer

Portfolio selection and finding the resting state of a magnet are, mathematically, the same problem. Each asset is represented by a small magnet that points up (held) or down (not held). An asset's expected return acts as a field pulling its magnet up, and the way two assets rise and fall together acts as a force between their magnets, so assets that crash together are discouraged from being held together. Every candidate portfolio then carries an energy, and the optimal portfolio is the configuration with the lowest one.

Ten demonstration assets occupy the ring, flipping under Metropolis dynamics while simulated annealing lowers the temperature: the search begins hot and random, then cools until only improvements survive. The lower trace shows the energy falling as the system settles, and the converged basket is reported with its expected return, volatility, and Sharpe ratio.

Set the portfolio size \(K\) and the risk aversion \(\lambda\), then press Optimize. Large \(\lambda\) makes the couplings dominate, so the optimizer gives up return to avoid assets that move together, while small \(\lambda\) favors return and tolerates the risk.

Formally this is a ground-state search of an Ising Hamiltonian: spins \(s_i \in \{-1,+1\}\) encode held/not-held, expected returns enter as local fields \(h_i\), the covariance matrix supplies the couplings \(J_{ij}\), and the cardinality target \(K\) is enforced by a quadratic penalty. Spins flip under Metropolis dynamics while the temperature follows an annealing schedule. The full application adds sector-exposure penalties and live market data, though its production constraint encoding stays proprietary.

\(H = -\sum_i h_i s_i + \lambda \sum_{i<j} J_{ij}\, s_i s_j + H_{\mathrm{constraints}}\)

Nickelate Fermiology

In 2019 researchers produced superconductivity in a nickel-based material for the first time. Its copper-based cousins, the cuprates, have held the high-temperature superconductivity record since the 1980s, and the mechanism behind them is still debated. If nickelates behaved as copies of cuprates, they would offer a second route into the same problem. My thesis showed the resemblance is only partial: nickelates carry an extra family of electrons that changes their behavior, and this simulation shows where it appears.

Electrons in a crystal fill up the available states like water filling a basin. The Fermi surface is the waterline: the boundary between filled and empty states, where conduction, magnetism, and superconductivity are decided. This map shows that waterline across the crystal's full Brillouin zone, and the bright contours mark where electrons sit at zero energy cost. The curve hugging the corners is the familiar cuprate-like band. The pocket that can open at the center (\(\Gamma\)) is the extra population, the focus of my published work.

Raise the chemical potential \(\mu\) slowly and watch a bright island appear at the center of the map: that is a second Fermi pocket opening. This event, absent in cuprates, redirects the material's magnetic and charge tendencies. Then increase \(V\) and watch the two electron families hybridize, opening gaps where their bands cross.

The map plots zero-frequency spectral weight \(A(\mathbf{k},0)\) for a two-band tight-binding model: a Ni \(d_{x^2-y^2}\) band with hopping \(t, t'\), plus a rare-earth-derived interlayer band, coupled by hybridization \(V\). Fermi-surface nesting of these sheets produces peaks in the spin and charge susceptibilities that seed the instabilities identified in JPCM 37, 125703 (2025) and developed in full in the thesis.

\(E_{\pm}(\mathbf{k}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2) \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2)^2 + V^2}\)

Moiré Interferometer

Two window screens overlaid at a slight angle produce large, slowly shifting ripples that exist in neither screen alone. The same happens with two one-atom-thick crystal sheets, where the ripples become a moiré superlattice: a pattern hundreds of atoms wide that the electrons genuinely feel. At a twist near 1.1°, called the magic angle, the pattern slows the electrons down, and slow electrons interact strongly. This is how twisted bilayer graphene was made to superconduct in 2018.

Two honeycomb lattices in additive light: cyan fixed, orange twisted by \(\theta\). The glowing beat pattern is the moiré superlattice, and the scale bar tracks its period \(\lambda\). Between my master's and PhD I studied this system, graphene on hexagonal boron nitride, with DFT, watching the superlattice reorganize the electronic bands.

Drag \(\theta\) toward zero and watch the period grow without bound. It diverges as the twist vanishes, which is why small angles make large patterns. The formula below the slider updates live.

The moiré period follows \(\lambda = a / 2\sin(\theta/2)\). A superlattice this large folds the Brillouin zone into a mini-zone, and near the magic angle the folded bands flatten: bandwidth \(W\) collapses while the Coulomb energy \(U\) stays fixed, so the correlation ratio \(U/W\) grows large. When interaction dominates kinetic energy, the electron liquid reorganizes: correlated insulators, unconventional superconductivity, and orbital magnetism all emerge from the same flat-band condition.

How I Work

Every project on this page, from nickelates to portfolios, follows the same four-step loop.

01

Reduce

Strip the system to the few variables that control it. A material becomes two bands, and a portfolio becomes spins and couplings. If this reduction is wrong, no amount of computation compensates for it.

02

Formulate

Cast the reduction into a structure with known machinery: a Hamiltonian, a variational ansatz, an optimization landscape. Solving it then means proving something rather than only running something.

03

Compute

Vectorize the loops, move the hot paths onto GPUs or clusters, and benchmark against exact baselines before trusting anything at scale. Speed turns a model from a diagram into an instrument that can be experimented with.

04

Validate

Test against experiment, market data, or exact solutions. A model has to survive contact with data it was not built on, and when it fails, the failure points to where the reduction was wrong.

Neural-Network Quantum States

Neural networks as compressed representations of quantum many-body states. The memory needed to store such a state doubles with every particle added and outgrows any computer within a few dozen particles, so a network learns a variational representation \(|\psi_\theta\rangle\) instead. I train these by variational Monte Carlo and validate them against exact diagonalization, with extensions toward thermal states, dynamics, and neutral-atom hardware.

validating

Ab Initio-Informed Battery Modeling

Connecting first-principles calculations to engineering-scale battery models. Atomistic quantities computed with DFT (lithium migration barriers, site capacities, open-circuit voltages) enter as physical priors into the DFN cell model engineers use. Where model and lab measurements disagree, that structured residual becomes the training signal for machine-learned lifetime prediction.

Benchmarking Quantum-Inspired Optimization

Quantum-inspired optimizers are often benchmarked under conventions that favor them. In my framework, classical and quantum-inspired methods face the same problems, the same metric, and the same exact baselines, so a measured gap reflects solver quality rather than a change in convention. Statistical Physics of Market Microstructure

Order books as driven spin systems. A market's order book is thousands of agents flipping between buy and sell while influencing their neighbors, which makes it an interacting spin system held far from equilibrium. I am investigating whether its collective regimes, and the moments they switch, carry predictive signal the way phase transitions do in physics.
